# v0.2.1: The new look

The screen, made over before milestone 3. The panels lose their boxes, much of what you point at explains itself, the game fits on a small screen, a few things move, and you can pick your colours. How the sprites live hasn't changed.

## What's new

- **Panels without boxes.** The inspector and the event log sit on soft shades instead of inside lines, and the map runs to the edges of the screen: 54 by 24 tiles at 100×30, where you saw 52 by 21. Text is coloured by its job: headings in cyan, gains in green, losses in red, keys in bold. The inspector's tabs sit at the right of its title row, and a click opens one.
- **Help for the screen you're on.** `?` opens the help at the page for where you are: the world, the inspector, the sprite list, the population graph or the family tree. Type to search every page at once. A key that would do nothing just now is greyed out in the hints rather than hidden, so they stop shuffling about.
- **A command palette.** `:` opens it: type what you want to do, such as `save`, and it lists what matches, each with its key, and `Enter` does it. Handy for a key you've forgotten.
- **Point at things.** Rest the mouse on a drive, a chemical, a gene or one of the log's filters, and a tooltip says what it is in a sentence: what makes a sprite hungry and what eases its hunger, say, or what a gene does. Anything that can explain itself is drawn in a soft blue in the default colours. Point at a sprite's name in the event log to light it up on the map, and click it to select it.
- **Small screens.** The game plays in a terminal as small as 20×5, giving up a part at a time: under 100 columns the inspector waits until `i` opens it over the map, under 30 rows the event log goes, and on the smallest screens the map is drawn alone. It isn't much of a view at 20×5, but it works. The title screen needs 60×20; on a smaller screen, start with `--seed 7`, say, to go straight into a world.
- **A bigger inspector.** `i` makes the inspector fill the screen, its lines in two or three columns, and `PgUp` and `PgDn` turn its pages. `i` again, or `Esc`, brings the map back.
- **Square tiles.** `o` draws each tile two characters wide, so a square patch of grass looks square.
- **Motion.** A death flashes and leaves a ghost that fades to grey. An egg flashes faster and faster in its last 100 ticks, then its shell cracks and splits as the hatchling climbs out. A drive's bar surges when it rises fast, and fills a third of a block at a time (`░▒▓█`), so you can see it move. Tabs sweep in, pop-ups fade, the view glides over to a sprite, and a faint trail follows the one you've selected. Most of it calms down at 4× and up, and `--no-motion`, or the settings screen, turns it all off.
- **Palettes.** Five sets of colours: the default, high contrast, colour-blind (never red against green), mono (bold and reverse video only), and Game Kid, greens on black, like an old handheld's screen. The characters are chosen separately from the colours: the usual ones (CP437), plain ASCII, or a theme file of your own for either. With `NO_COLOR` set, the game starts in mono, unless you pick colours yourself.
- **A settings screen.** `Ctrl+T` opens it: glyphs, colours, motion and square tiles, a row each. What you pick is saved in `settings.ron` in the game's folder, so you only choose once.
- **Removing it cleanly.** These notes, and each download's `README.txt`, end with how to remove the game and everything it wrote, and the `README.txt` links to this release's version of the manual.

## Fixed

- The look you pick now stays with you on the title screen and into your next world. It used to switch back to the one the game started with.
- The `F3` overlay's box no longer lets the pointer through. Pointing at it moved the Cursor to the tile underneath, and a click on it landed on the map.

## Before you upgrade

- **Your worlds carry on.** Saves and autosaves from v0.2.0 load as before. A world from v0.2.0 keeps the game's data it started with, though, so pointing at a drive or a chemical in it says only what kind of thing it is. Start a new world for the full explanations.
- **Replays from v0.2.0** play only in v0.2.0, and the first v0.2.1 session writes over the last session's replay. Copy `last_session.replay` out of the game's folder first if you want to keep it.
- **`Ctrl+T` opens the settings screen,** where it opened the theme menu. The same choices are there, with motion and square tiles beside them.
- **`--ascii` changes only the characters now,** so on its own it draws ASCII in the default colours, with shaded panels, and with `--theme` it draws your theme's colours in ASCII. Pick high contrast or mono on the settings screen for lines between the panels again.
- **A theme file of your own from v0.2.0** still loads, and draws lines between the panels until you add a `panels` section for shades. Put it in the `themes` folder and pick it on both the Glyphs and the Colours rows to use both its characters and its colours.

## Known problems

- Grown sprites die of hunger or thirst, and try to eat while too full, a little more often than planned, and partners keep each other in sight a little under half the time ([#332](https://github.com/Keazra/terra-sprites/issues/332)).
- Over many generations, sprites evolve to live more than twice as long, but not to do better: in a fresh world, the evolved ones raise fewer young than the first sprites' genes do ([#332](https://github.com/Keazra/terra-sprites/issues/332)).
- Resting never counts as relief from tiredness, so sprites can't learn that it helps ([#125](https://github.com/Keazra/terra-sprites/issues/125)).
- The downloads aren't signed, so Windows or a Mac may balk the first time (the zip's `README.txt` says what to do), and Windows with Smart App Control on won't run it at all: install it with Rust there instead ([#352](https://github.com/Keazra/terra-sprites/issues/352)).
- On a terminal with a light background, such as a Mac's Terminal in light mode, the panels are dark and their writing hard to read. Press `Ctrl+T` and pick mono for Colours, or use a dark terminal profile ([#403](https://github.com/Keazra/terra-sprites/issues/403)).
- In the sprite list's filter, `#1` finds every first-generation sprite too, not only Sprite #1 ([#335](https://github.com/Keazra/terra-sprites/issues/335)).
- Loading a save hides the `F3` overlay until you press `F3` again ([#336](https://github.com/Keazra/terra-sprites/issues/336)).
- On a keyboard that isn't US English, such as a German one, `:` and `?` can stop opening the command palette and the help if you let go of Shift before the key. Press it once more and let go of the key before Shift, and they work again ([#408](https://github.com/Keazra/terra-sprites/issues/408)).
- On a screen only 5 or 6 rows tall, `:` opens the command palette without showing it, and it takes what you type until you press `Esc` ([#404](https://github.com/Keazra/terra-sprites/issues/404)).

## Install

Download the zip for your computer from this page's Assets, below: Windows, Mac (Apple silicon) or Linux. Each has a `README.txt` that says how to run it, and, on a Mac, how to open it the first time, since it comes from a developer Apple doesn't know. Or, with Rust 1.97.1 or newer:

    cargo install --git https://github.com/Keazra/terra-sprites --tag v0.2.1 --locked terra-tui

Either way, it's best in a terminal of 100×30 or more (on Windows, Windows Terminal), though it plays on a smaller one.

## Uninstall

Delete the folder you unzipped, the zip if you kept it, and on Linux `terra-sprites` from the folder on your PATH if you moved it there. Or, if you installed it with Rust, run `cargo uninstall terra-tui`. Your worlds and settings stay in the game's own folder until you delete that too, so a newer version finds them:

- Windows (PowerShell): `Remove-Item -Recurse "$env:APPDATA\terra-sprites"`
- Mac: `rm -rf ~/Library/Application\ Support/terra-sprites`
- Linux: `rm -rf ~/.local/share/terra-sprites`, or `$XDG_DATA_HOME/terra-sprites` if you've set that

The game writes nothing anywhere else. A Rust install also leaves the code it downloaded in Cargo's folder: the manual's [Uninstalling](https://github.com/Keazra/terra-sprites/blob/v0.2.1/docs/manual.md#uninstalling) says how to clear it.
